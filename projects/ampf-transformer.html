Georgia Tech Advanced Manufacturing Pilot Facility

Ramblin’ Wreck ↔ Buzz Transformer

Designed and optimized mechanical transformation systems for a large-scale structure that converts Georgia Tech’s Ramblin’ Wreck into Buzz.

Undergraduate Research Assistant · February 2025 – December 2025

SolidWorks Mechanism Design ~75% Mass Reduction Design for Manufacturing

Solving the Transformation

The challenge was not simply modeling a car and a mascot. Each component needed a physical path between the two configurations, with room to move and geometry that could actually be manufactured.

I used SolidWorks to develop the transformation architecture, working through moving-part clearances, component interference, and mechanical packaging. Three areas drove much of the mechanism development:

Rear-Stowed Buzz Head

The front of the vehicle was too narrow for a proportioned head. I developed a rear-storage concept that let the head slide through rectangular cutouts into its deployed position, with pins considered for locking and a cover panel over the storage area.

Retracting Roof

The roof could not remain projecting out of Buzz. I developed a concept that slid it down into the vehicle body, improving clearance and the final silhouette.

Arm & Shoulder Motion

I explored rotation about an axis approximately parallel to the vehicle’s kingpin axis, along with a telescoping elbow-pin concept to reposition components into Buzz’s arms.

These were mechanism-development concepts, rather than a claim that all three became final fabricated mechanisms.

A Lighter Structure

~60 kg → ~15 kg

Transformer design mass before and after lightweighting.

~75% Reduction

Less mass to move, with reduced material use and manufacturing cost as design goals.

I removed material from overly thick regions and introduced non-circular lightening cutouts in lower-stress areas, aiming to preserve important structural regions and load paths. Lightweight concepts also included carbon-fiber square rods and tubes.

Designing Beyond the CAD Model

CNC Machining Metal Powder Bed Fusion Wire EDM Polymer Prototyping

I balanced component detail against process capabilities, achievable tolerances, material usage, and cost. For the head-storage cover, wire EDM was considered to achieve a precise fit and a flush surrounding surface.

After the SolidWorks design was largely complete, the project moved into polymer prototyping to validate geometry and transformation concepts before committing to more expensive metal manufacturing.
